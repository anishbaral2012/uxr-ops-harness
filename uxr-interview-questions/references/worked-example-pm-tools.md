# Worked Example: Generative Discovery Research

This is a complete reference example for the `uxr-interview-questions` skill. It shows the format, depth, and consistency the skill aims to produce. Use it as a template; do not copy verbatim.

**Input Research Plan (what the user supplied):**

> We're a B2B SaaS company building project management tooling for engineering teams. We want to understand how engineering leads currently track and communicate project status across stakeholders. Sessions are 60 min, semi-structured. Participants: engineering leads or managers at companies 50–500 people who manage 3+ direct reports and run at least one active project.
>
> **Hypotheses:** (H1) Status updates are mostly manual and time-consuming. (H2) Existing tools don't aggregate across systems. (H3) Stakeholders want different levels of detail than what tools provide by default.

---

## Study Overview

**Objective:** Understand how engineering leads track and communicate project status, and where current tools/workflows fall short — before designing new solutions.
**Method:** Generative discovery, semi-structured interviews
**Participants:** Engineering leads/managers, 50–500 person companies, 3+ direct reports, 1+ active projects
**Session Length:** 60 minutes
**Hypotheses being tested:**
- H1: Status updates are mostly manual and time-consuming.
- H2: Existing tools don't aggregate across systems.
- H3: Stakeholders want different levels of detail than tools provide.

> **Moderator Note:** Resist the urge to mention your product or category. This is about their world. Watch for workarounds — they signal unmet needs.

---

## Screener Questions

**S1. What best describes your current role?**
- [ ] Individual contributor — *Disqualify*
- [ ] Engineering lead / tech lead — *Qualify*
- [ ] Engineering manager — *Qualify*
- [ ] Director / VP of Engineering — *Disqualify*

**S2. How many direct reports do you currently manage?**
- [ ] 0 — *Disqualify*
- [ ] 1–2 — *Disqualify*
- [ ] 3–6 — *Qualify*
- [ ] 7+ — *Qualify*

**S3. How many active projects or workstreams are you currently tracking?**
- [ ] 0 — *Disqualify*
- [ ] 1–2 — *Borderline*
- [ ] 3–5 — *Qualify*
- [ ] 6+ — *Qualify*

**S4. Which tools does your team use for project tracking? (Select all)**
- [ ] Jira / Linear / Asana / GitHub Projects / Notion / Spreadsheets / None

*Any answer qualifies.*

**S5. How often do you give project status updates to stakeholders outside your team?**
- [ ] Rarely / never — *Disqualify*
- [ ] Once a month — *Borderline*
- [ ] Weekly — *Qualify*
- [ ] Multiple times a week — *Qualify*

## Intro & Consent `[~3 min]`

> Use approved study-specific consent language. Confirm actual recording, transcription, AI processing, access, sharing, retention, withdrawal and deletion practices before the session. Obtain separate permission to record; do not promise terms that have not been verified.

---

## Warm-Up `[~5 min]`

**1. To get us started — can you tell me a bit about your team and what you're currently working on?**
   - How long have you been in this role?
   - Has the team size changed recently?

**2. Walk me through a typical Monday morning — what's the first thing you do when you sit down?**
   - Is that pretty consistent week to week?
   - What kicks the week into gear?

---

## Core Questions `[~37 min]`

### Theme 1: Project Tracking & Awareness `[~13 min]`

**Q1. Walk me through how you keep track of everything happening across your team right now.**

*Why: Establish the full ecosystem before zooming into any one tool.*
- What's your "source of truth" for project status?
- How do you know if something is off track before it becomes a problem?

**Q2. Tell me about a time a project got into trouble and you didn't see it coming.**
- How did you find out?
- What would you have needed to catch it earlier?

**Q3. How do you decide what to pay close attention to vs. what you trust your team to handle?**
- Has that changed as your team has grown?
- What's the cost when you get that balance wrong?

### Theme 2: Stakeholder Communication `[~13 min]`

**Q4. Describe how you typically communicate project status to people outside your team.**
- Who asks you for updates the most?
- Do you have a set cadence, or is it reactive?

**Q5. Tell me about a status update that went particularly well — what made it work?**
- What did you include that others don't usually?
- What did the audience actually do with it?

**Q6. And tell me about one that didn't go well.**
- What was missing?
- What did you have to scramble to fix afterward?

**Q7. How much time would you estimate you spend per week preparing or giving status updates?**
- Does that feel like the right amount?
- What part takes the longest?

### Theme 3: Tools & Workarounds `[~11 min]`

**Q8. Show me — or describe — the setup you use today to manage project visibility.**

*Why: Workarounds reveal unmet needs.*
- What's held together with duct tape that you wish worked better?
- If you had to recommend this setup to a peer, what caveats would you give?

**Q9. When you need a piece of information that lives in a different tool than where you're working, what do you do?**
- How often does that happen in a typical day?
- What's your workaround?

---

## Closing `[~5 min]`

**1. If you could change one thing about how project status and communication works on your team, what would it be?**

**2. Of everything we talked about today, what feels most pressing to you right now?**

**3. Is there anything we haven't touched on that you think is important?**

**4. Is there anyone who handles this very differently from you — in a way you admire or find interesting? *(Anyone we should talk to?)***

**5. On a scale of 1–10, how comfortable did this conversation feel? Anything that would have made it better?**

> *Thank the participant. Explain next steps and the confirmed withdrawal process. Ask if they have questions.*

**Time totals:** 3 (intro) + 5 (warm-up) + 37 (core) + 5 (closing) + 10 (transitions/buffer) = **60 min** ✓
**Probe count:** uniformly 2 follow-ups per main question across warm-up and all core questions.
**Core question count:** 9 (within the 9–12 budget for 60 min).
