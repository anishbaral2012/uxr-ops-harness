# Research Plan for Library Room Booking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Date | 20 November 2026 |
| Researcher | Demo research team |

## Background

The fictional library network is designing a mobile booking flow for small study rooms. Patrons in the scenario use a branch phone line, a service desk or a basic web form.

### Primary goals for this product or feature

Help a patron find an available room, decide whether it suits the group and tell others where to meet.

### Problem statement

The product lead needs to decide which information belongs before room selection and what the reservation should tell the organizer. The brief contains no observed booking journeys.

### Target audience

Adult patrons who arranged or tried to arrange a study room for two to six people in the last three months, including patrons who stopped before booking.

## Research Objectives

1. We want to understand what patrons settle first when arranging a room in order to decide how to start the search.
2. We want to identify the information patrons check before committing in order to decide what to show in search results.
3. We want to understand how organizers pass along booking details in order to scope confirmation and sharing.

## Research Questions

**RQ1.** What do room organizers settle first when searching for space?
**RQ2.** What room information do organizers consult before committing?
**RQ3.** How do organizers confirm the reservation and tell their group where to go?

## Methodology

**Method.** Semi structured discovery interviews about the most recent booking attempt. Use concrete examples to examine choices and workarounds before a prototype exists.

**Format.** Remote, moderated, 30 minutes, with one optional clarification conversation. Record the attempt, booking channel and observed gaps in the account.

**Sample and unit of analysis.** Six distinct organizers, three first time and three repeat organizers, with coverage of web, phone and desk channels. Analyze by participant and keep sessions as separate sources. This purposive sample is designed to surface plausible patterns and exceptions.

## Participants

| Attribute | Criteria |
|---|---|
| User type | Adult library patron organizing a study group of two to six |
| Experience level | Three first time and three repeat room organizers |
| Device and channel | Include phone, desk and mobile web booking attempts |
| Key screener question | In the last three months, did you arrange or try to arrange a public library study room for a group of two to six? |

**Exclude.** Non organizers and people whose only example falls outside the three month window. Keep abandoned attempts in scope.

**Consent.** A real study would require approved information and consent materials before collection. This exercise uses invented sources.

### Research operations and data handling

The demo research lead owns the guide and source review. The demo product lead owns the prototype decision on 20 November. For a real study, recruit through approved library channels, offer accessible remote or phone sessions and pilot the script with one eligible organizer. Confirm compensation, staffing, scheduling and accommodations before invitation.

A real study would also require confirmed consent, recording choices, transcription and AI processing terms, approved storage and access, and procedures for withdrawal, retention and deletion. No participant contact details or consent records exist for this exercise.

## Risks and Limitations

Recruiting only through the web form could miss people who call or give up. A real study would recruit through the desk as well.

Room searches are recalled after the fact. Anchoring on the most recent attempt and asking what happened next can help, but a later prototype test would be needed to assess task completion.

One optional follow up can clarify a case. Count the participant once and document the additional question. The fictional interviews cannot inform actual service decisions.
