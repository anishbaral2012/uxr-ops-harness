# Qualitative Analysis of Library Room Booking

Synthetic exercise. The six participant profiles, seven sources and all quotations are fictional. This report demonstrates how to link raw evidence to design proposals for an initial prototype.

Six fictional organizers were represented across seven sessions on 17 and 18 November. The follow up with P6 clarified what happened after sharing. It does not add a participant to any count. All six main excerpts cover RQ1 through RQ3.

In the second cycle, we compared fixed time with fixed branch, grouped capacity, equipment and access inquiries as checks driven by a room requirement, and grouped written, verbal and printed booking details by how they helped organizers brief their group. We checked each cluster against the cases that took a different path.

## T1 A fixed meeting time narrowed the search for three organizers

**Insight.** P1, P3 and P5 settled on a time before considering branches. P2, P4 and P6 fixed the branch because of travel constraints and had room to shift the time.

**Evidence.** Three of six reported a fixed time as their starting point. Three of six gave a branch constraint. We coded the item the organizer committed to first, based on the account in Q1 and Q2.

**Quote.** “Tuesday at four was the only time all four of us could do. I checked two branches for that hour.” (P1, organizer, S01 line 3). The group had already eliminated other times.

**Counter-evidence.** P2 changed the meeting day to stay at East Branch. P4 prioritized the bus route and P6 prioritized a branch their group could walk to. All six discussed the search constraint.

**Implication.** The first search choice needs to accommodate different constraints across these accounts.

## T2 A specific room need prompted checks before booking

**Insight.** Four organizers asked about capacity, equipment or physical access before making a commitment. Two of those organizers left the web path to call about capacity.

**Evidence.** Four of six explicitly checked a room attribute before committing in their Q3 account (P1, P2, P4, P6). The code requires a stated check of a room attribute, rather than simply selecting a free slot.

**Quote.** “I couldn't tell whether five of us would fit. I stopped at the form and called the branch to ask.” (P6, organizer, S06 line 4). The missing capacity information prompted a switch of channel in this case.

**Quote.** “I asked if there was a way to reach the room without stairs before I booked it. I would have tried another room if the answer was no.” (P4, organizer, S04 line 4). Route suitability was a condition for that booking.

**Counter-evidence.** P3 took the librarian's suggestion. P5 already knew the room and checked only for an opening. All six discussed selection.

**Implication.** Suitability information can matter before a time slot becomes a booking, especially when a group has a specific requirement.

## T3 Booking details gave organizers something to pass along

**Insight.** Four organizers used a confirmation, booking slip or details supplied by staff when telling their group where to meet.

**Evidence.** Four of six described reusing a booking detail in Q4 and Q5 (P1, P2, P3, P6). The criterion is explicit reuse of booking information in the handoff.

**Quote.** “I forwarded the confirmation because it had the branch and room number together. Nobody had to ask which building.” (P1, organizer, S01 line 5). A single reference contained both location details in that account.

**Quote.** “I used the booking slip to tell the group which floor to meet on.” (P3, organizer, S03 line 5). The useful detail came from a printed artifact.

**Counter-evidence.** P4 told a group that was already present. P5 sent only an update because the group knew the room. Everyone discussed sharing. P6's follow up shows that supplying time and room can still leave the entrance unclear.

**Implication.** A reservation can serve as a reference for the organizer and the rest of the group. The information it needs depends on what recipients already know.

## Opportunity Areas

**Search that supports either constraint (T1, T2).** A time or a branch may already be fixed. Once a suitable slot appears, the room needs to fit the group.

**Room details from choice through arrival (T2, T3).** Organizers in the sample asked for suitability facts before booking and needed location details afterward. Staff provided some of both.

## Strategic Recommendations

| Priority | Evidence chain | Prototype proposal | What to check next |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | RQ1 and T1. People work from either a fixed meeting time or a fixed branch. | Allow either date and time or branch as the first filter. | Observe both search scenarios in prototype sessions. |
| High | RQ2 and T2. Capacity and access can determine whether a room works for the group. | Show capacity, equipment and known access details before booking. | Confirm which details staff can maintain, then test if users can find them. |
| Medium | RQ3 and T3. Organizers reuse booking details to brief the group. | Try a confirmation summary with branch, room, floor, date and time that organizers can copy. | Check what a recipient can infer from the shared summary. |

These priorities illustrate prototype decisions within the fictional scenario.

## Appendix Insights

**Entrance details, one case.** “One friend asked which entrance to use. I sent a map pin because the building has doors on two streets.” (P6, organizer, S07 line 3). Only P6 was asked this follow up. Test whether entrance details matter in branches with more than one public entrance.

## Gaps and Limitations

The exercise uses six invented session accounts with one follow up. The counts describe the fixture. Retrospective interview methods would not establish task success, live availability accuracy or the prevalence of these behaviors. The sample covers adult room organizers and three channels. It does not capture the library staff workflow or disabled patrons' experiences directly. A real prototype decision would require actual research.
