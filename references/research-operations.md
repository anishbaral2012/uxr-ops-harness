# Research operations and participant data

Use this checklist with the responsible researcher and the organization's approved policies. Do not treat it as legal advice or claim the workflow enforces any policy.

## Before recruitment

- Name the decision owner, research owner, decision date, target population, sampling rationale, eligibility criteria, coverage goals, recruitment channels, budget, compensation, scheduling, accommodations, and backup plan. Pilot the materials. Keep recruitment contact details in an approved restricted system, separately from analysis files.
- Confirm the study's purpose and permitted uses, approved consent language, recording choices, transcription provider, any AI processing and providers, storage locations, access, sharing, retention, deletion and withdrawal procedures, and the person responsible for executing them. Apply the organization's review process for the study and jurisdictions involved. Do not invent terms or promise a specific right, time limit or processing location.
- Explain actual processing to participants in accessible language. Obtain and record the required consent and separate permission for recording. State what happens if recording is declined. Allow questions to be skipped and sessions stopped. Explain confirmed withdrawal or deletion conditions, limits, contact details and deadlines. Do not collect new data until unresolved essential handling decisions are resolved.

## During and after collection

- Use stable participant codes. Keep the identity-to-code key, consent records and scheduling details separately in an approved restricted location. Collect only necessary data. Pseudonyms, role, location and quotations may still identify a person.
- Track recruitment, attendance, cancellations, consent status, compensation and accommodations using the organization's approved systems. The local manifest contains no names, contact details, consent forms, or unnecessary demographic data. A code in a manifest is not proof of consent.
- Before sending data to a model or third party, confirm the actual provider, permitted data, access and retention arrangements, participant disclosure and organizational authorization. Review materials for identifiers and minimize content. Do not assume that a prompt alone enforces access or deletion.
- Limit report attributes and quotes to what is necessary and permitted. Review combinations for re-identification. Document retention owners and execute the confirmed deletion or withdrawal process across source files, exports and derived artifacts where applicable. Reassess findings and artifacts affected by withdrawn data. Do not claim deletion until verified.

## Method-specific branches

- Interviews and usability: document moderation, task context, observations, prompts, deviations and missing sessions.
- Diary studies: define cadence, duration, missing entries and participant burden; analyze entries by participant and time, not as independent people.
- Surveys, tree tests and card sorts: document the instrument, recruitment, exclusions, denominator and analysis appropriate to the design. Preserve raw data separately and avoid unsupported representativeness or precision claims.
- Experiments and analytics: define metrics, allocation or comparison, instrumentation, exclusions, assumptions and analysis plan before interpreting results. Estimate sample requirements or precision for the actual decision. Engage the appropriate quantitative expertise.

Report what is unknown, unverified, out of scope, or still requires human review.
